Six lasting institutions, not one-off campaigns.

Each area of our work is run as a permanent institution. Here is what that looks like on the ground, from healthcare and education to rural development.

Healthcare & Medicare

Care where it is needed most.

Free and subsidised treatment through charitable hospitals and mobile dispensaries. In 2025 the Foundation opened a new 250-bed Manav Charitable Hospital super-specialty, with six modular operating theatres, a 40-bed ICU and emergency department, and round-the-clock laboratory, radiology and pharmacy services.

Specialties now span cardiology, nephrology, urology, gastroenterology, pulmonology, neurology and oncology, alongside the Jindal Naturecure Institute, Jindal Charitable Hospital and the H. S. Bansal Charitable Hospital in Delhi.

Education

Schools and colleges that open doors.

Jindal Public School, Bengaluru, established in 1972, teaches over 2,300 students and recorded a 100% pass rate; a second campus opened at Dabaspet in 2023. The Jindal Pre-University College and the Jindal College for Women together educate close to 2,000 students, from commerce and science to computer applications.

Scholarships

Removing the cost barrier.

Merit and need-based scholarships for students from economically weaker sections, awarded since 1969. In 2025-26 the Foundation disbursed ₹16.29 crore to 8,580 students, with monthly support from ₹500 to ₹5,000 depending on the course.

Rural & Social Development

Dignity in everyday life.

School buildings, sanitation and potable water across 175 villages in Karnataka, and free nutritious meals every day through Jan Bhojnalaya for economically weaker families in Bengaluru and Dabaspet.

Women Empowerment

Skills that become livelihoods.

The Mahila Arts & Crafts Charitable Institute offers free vocational training in tailoring, embroidery, computer skills and more, with a monthly stipend during training and tools provided on completion so graduates can begin earning.

Social Reform

Healthier communities.

Health-awareness drives, free dental and medical camps, and campaigns against the use of intoxicants, carried out through the Foundation's health museum, publications and outreach programmes.
